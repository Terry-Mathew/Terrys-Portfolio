# Terry Mathew — Bio

Terry Mathew works across data products, analytics, commercial systems, and applied AI.

His career did not follow a straight line.

He started in hospitality at Four Seasons Hotel Mumbai, later worked in retail at Bose, moved into enterprise technical support at IBM, and then into HR shared services and process improvement at Amazon.

He joined Oracle in 2018 in business operations.

At Oracle, his work gradually moved from processing commercial transactions and supporting sales teams to handling complex cloud deals, leading an operations team, building analytics, and eventually owning data product and partner analytics workstreams.

That progression gave him experience on both sides of a data problem. He has worked inside the operational process that creates the data and later worked on the analytics and products used to understand that process.

From March 2024, Terry has been Senior Data Product Manager for Partner Analytics at Oracle. He is currently in that role, and his planned final working day at Oracle is 14 October 2026.

His work included defining requirements, data and metric logic, delivery priorities, reporting requirements, user acceptance testing, and operational workflows with Finance, Engineering, Sales Operations, Revenue Operations, and partner-program stakeholders.

Some of the problems he worked on involved partner credit management, business and technical assessments, incentive eligibility, cloud consumption, bookings, deal registration, and commercial operations.

Terry is now interested in work where data products are treated as products rather than simply as reporting outputs.

He is also building applied AI projects independently. His current projects include the Digital Twin on his portfolio, a Product Discovery AI system, a Deep Research Agent, and Settle, a personal-finance decision simulator.

The common thread across his professional and personal projects is not a particular industry or tool. He tends to work on situations where the process is complicated, the information is fragmented, or people need a clearer way to make a decision.
